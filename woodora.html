Woodora – Furniture

Product Overview

  • Woodora is a modern furniture e-commerce platform built to make premium, handcrafted wooden furniture easy to discover and shop.
  • The project explores how a furniture brand can feel warm while still handling the practical needs of browsing by category, pieces, and buying with confidence.
  • The experience takes users from an inviting first impression, through category-based discovery and best-selling picks, toward a simple, frictionless purchase.
Role UI/UX Designer
Client Lukas Schneider
Duration 3 Weeks
Live Site Woodora.design

Problem Statement

Furniture shopping online often feels either too cluttered with options or too generic to trust with a real purchase.

The challenge was to create a digital experience that could:

  • Organize a wide range of furniture into clear, browsable categories
  • Make product discovery feel effortless, not overwhelming
  • Build enough trust for someone to buy furniture without seeing it in person
  • Encourage repeat visits through offers and a recognizable brand feel

Core Problem

How might we help someone go from browsing furniture to feeling confident enough to add it to their cart?

Possible Solution

I designed Woodora as a clean, category-first furniture store that pairs strong visuals with a simple path to purchase.

The website communicates this through:

  • A warm, inviting hero showcasing the product directly
  • Clear categories for different spaces (home, office, hospital, school)
  • A best-sellers section to guide undecided shoppers
  • A membership offer to encourage early commitment
  • Trust-building sections like warranty, delivery, and support

User Research

The research focused on what makes furniture shoppers comfortable buying online instead of visiting a physical store.

  • What makes a furniture site feel trustworthy?
  • How do shoppers prefer to browse — by room, by product type, or both?
  • What convinces someone to buy furniture without touching it first?
  • What makes a shopper come back and buy again?

Key Findings

Categories Reduce Decision Fatigue

Grouping furniture by space (home, office, hospital, school) helps shoppers find relevant pieces faster.

Visuals Do the Selling

For furniture, strong product photography matters more than attractive titles and long descriptions.

Offers Build Momentum

A visible discount or membership deal nudges hesitant shoppers toward a clean decision.

Trust Signals Close the Sale

Warranty, delivery, money-back guarantee, and support details reassure buyers making a bigger purchase.

User Persona

The user persona represents a typical Woodora shopper furnishing a space and looking for quality furniture they can trust to buy online. This persona helps define the user's motivations, expectations, goals, and pain points throughout the digital shopping experience.

Goals

  • Find furniture that suits their space and style.
  • Compare a few solid options without endless scrolling.
  • Get a good deal without compromising quality.
  • Feel confident buying without seeing the product in person.

Pain Points

  • Hard to tell quality apart from photos alone.
  • Unclear delivery and warranty terms.
  • Too many similar products with no clear standout picks.
  • Manual spreadsheet tracking to compare options.

Background

Farah frequently sources furniture for both her home and client projects. She values clean design, reliable delivery, and a shop she can return to without second-guessing quality each time.

User Empathy Map

The empathy map captures Farah's thoughts, words, behaviors, and emotions while browsing for furniture online. It helps identify what influences her decisions and where the digital experience can better support her.

Thinks

  • Will this actually match my space?
  • Is this worth the price compared to elsewhere?
  • Can I trust the delivery timeline?
  • What happens if something arrives damaged?

Says

  • I want to see more angles of this piece.
  • Is there a warranty on this?
  • I like that they have a category just for offices.
  • Let me check the discount before I decide.

Does

  • Browses by category first, then narrows down.
  • Compares two or three similar products.
  • Checks warranty and delivery details before buying.
  • Signs up for offers before making a decision.

Feels

  • Reassured by clear categories and honest photos.
  • Encouraged by visible discounts and offers.
  • Confident when warranty and support are clearly stated.
  • Hesitant when product details feel incomplete.

Typography

The typography was chosen to feel warm and approachable while keeping product details and prices easy to scan. The heading type gives the brand a clean, modern presence, while the body type keeps longer descriptions and specs readable.

Used Colors

The visual system uses a warm, natural palette suited to a furniture brand. A soft accent reinforces the handcrafted, homely feel, while light neutral tones keep the layout airy and product-focused rather than heavy or corporate.

The wireframing stage established the single-page structure, content hierarchy, and rhythm before applying the final visual style. It helped organize the flow from hero, features, and workflow through pricing and social proof into one uninterrupted journey.

User Interface

The final landing page brings everything together — warm typography, a wood-inspired color palette, and a clear path from browsing to buying. The sections below walk through the actual designed screens, section by section.

01 -

Warm Welcome, Instant Browse

The hero pairs a soft product shot with a search bar placed right below it, letting shoppers start browsing furniture the moment they land instead of scrolling to find it.

02 -

Shop By Space, Not Just Category

Categories are split by real-world spaces — home, office, hospital, school — with a sub-category row underneath for shoppers who already know exactly what piece they want.

03 -

An Offer Worth Stopping For

A 50% membership discount sits right above the best-selling products, giving hesitant shoppers a reason to commit before they've even finished browsing.

04 -

Built On Craft, Not Just Catalog

The About section pairs a warm image collage with Woodora's story, positioning the brand around quality wood craftsmanship rather than just another product listing.

05 -

Reasons To Trust, Reasons To Return

Five short trust points — care, delivery, service, payment, warranty — sit above social links, reassuring shoppers before checkout and inviting them to follow along after.

06 -

Furniture In Its Real Setting

A flagship video gallery shows pieces styled in actual rooms, helping shoppers picture furniture in a real space rather than judging it from a plain product shot.

07 -

Real Buyers, Real Locations

Client feedback sits alongside physical shop listings, blending online trust signals with the reassurance of real, visitable stores nearby.

08 -

A Direct Line, Not Just a Form

The contact section pairs a simple message form with direct address, email, and phone details, so shoppers can reach out however feels most comfortable.

09 -

One Last Invitation

The closing CTA circles back to the brand's core promise — comfort meeting elegance — with one clear action before the footer closes the page.

What This Project Taught Me

01

Products Should Lead

For a furniture brand, letting the product image carry the message worked better than heavy text — people decide with their eyes first.

02

Categories Reduce Friction

Splitting furniture by space instead of one long catalog made browsing feel guided instead of overwhelming, even with a wide product range.

03

Warmth Builds Trust Too

For a home-focused brand, credibility isn't only about clean UI. Color, imagery, and tone all play a part in making the shop feel inviting.
