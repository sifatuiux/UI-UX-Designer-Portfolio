Stratum – Aethel

Product Overview

  • AETHEL is a conceptual luxury watch e-commerce experience designed to combine premium brand storytelling with a clear and intuitive shopping journey.
  • The project explores how a luxury product can be presented digitally without relying on excessive visual decoration.
  • The experience guides users from discovering the brand to understanding craftsmanship, exploring watches, building trust, and making a purchase decision.
  • The design balances luxury, storytelling, product clarity, and conversion within a single-page experience.
Role UI/UX Designer
Client Aethel Timepiece Co.
Duration 4 Weeks
Live Site Aethel.Design

Problem Statement

Luxury watch websites often prioritize visual presentation, but users also need enough information and reassurance to confidently evaluate a high-value product.

The challenge was to create an experience that could:

  • Communicate the premium character of the brand
  • Explain craftsmanship and product specifications clearly
  • Help users compare different watches
  • Show how the watches fit into real-life situations
  • Establish trust through authenticity, warranty, and packaging
  • Reduce uncertainty before purchase

Core Problem

How might we create a luxury watch shopping experience that feels emotionally premium while remaining informative, trustworthy, and easy to navigate?

Possible Solution

The proposed solution was a story-driven e-commerce landing page that combines emotional storytelling with practical product information.

Key solution areas included:

  • Strong hero section to establish the brand and product immediately
  • Craftsmanship & specifications to communicate product value
  • Product comparison cards to simplify product exploration
  • Lifestyle imagery to demonstrate real-world use
  • Packaging & authenticity section to increase purchase confidence
  • Customer testimonials to provide social proof

This approach allows users to first develop an emotional connection with the product and then receive the information needed to make a confident decision.

User Research

The research focused on understanding what users need when evaluating a premium watch online.

  • What creates trust in an online luxury purchase?
  • What product information influences decision-making?
  • How do users compare multiple watches?
  • What makes a luxury website feel premium rather than complicated?
  • What concerns might prevent users from purchasing online?

Key Findings

Trust First

For high-value products, users need reassurance around authenticity, warranty, quality, and after-sales support before purchasing.

Visual Clarity

High-quality imagery can attract users, but clear specifications and product details help them move toward a decision.

Product Context

Lifestyle photography helps users imagine how a watch looks and feels when worn rather than viewing it as an isolated product.

Compare Easily

When several products are available, users benefit from seeing key differences without opening multiple product pages.

Effortless Luxury

Users expect luxury interfaces to feel refined and spacious, but navigation and information should remain simple and predictable.

Trusted Voices

Testimonials and customer experiences can reduce uncertainty and reinforce the perceived value of the product.

User Persona

The user persona represents a typical AETHEL customer who values craftsmanship, personal style, and confidence when purchasing a premium watch online. This persona helps define the user's motivations, expectations, goals, and pain points throughout the digital shopping experience.

Goals

  • Find a watch that reflects his personal style and professional identity.
  • Understand the craftsmanship, materials, and specifications of each model.
  • Compare different watches before making a decision.
  • Feel confident about the quality and value of his purchase.

Pain Points

  • Difficulty comparing different watch models on some luxury websites.
  • Important specifications may be scattered across different sections.
  • Limited lifestyle context makes it difficult to imagine wearing the watch.
  • Lack of customer experiences can make an expensive purchase feel uncertain.

Typography

The typography system was designed to create a balance between luxury, elegance, and usability. A classic serif is used for prominent section titles, while a modern sans-serif handles the rest of the interface for clear and consistent readability.

Used Colors

The AETHEL color system uses a refined palette of soft grays and deep blues. Light neutral tones create a clean and balanced foundation, while progressively darker blues add depth, contrast, and a sense of sophistication. Together, the colors create a calm, timeless, and premium visual identity.

Mid-Fidelity Wireframe

The wireframing stage focused on defining the landing page structure, content hierarchy, and user flow before applying the final visual design. It helped establish clear relationships between sections and ensure a smooth journey from product discovery to purchase decision.

User Interface

The final landing page pulls everything together — clean typography, a restrained color palette, immersive imagery, and a clear path from discovery to purchase.

01 -

First Impression, Full Commitment

Went with an oversized serif wordmark to set a premium tone right away. The rotating product viewer lets people preview other variants before they even scroll — one less reason to bounce.

02 -

Letting the Product Speak in Detail

Skipped the typical spec table for connector-line callouts straight off the product photo. It's closer to how a print catalog would show it, and it makes each detail feel intentional instead of just listed.

03 -

Guided Choice, Not a Catalog Dump

Three editions, enough info upfront (rating, price, colors) to compare without clicking in. For anyone still unsure, "Find Your Perfect Match" gives them a softer way forward instead of just leaving.

04 -

Selling the Lifestyle, Not the Object

Moved the watch off the pedestal and into real moments — a steering wheel, a climb, a boardroom. The overlapping cards give it a bit of a lookbook feel rather than a plain gallery.

05 -

Unboxing Is Part of the Product

At this price point, what's inside the box matters almost as much as the watch. Showing the packaging directly answers the quiet doubt a lot of online buyers have — will this actually feel premium in hand?

06 -

Trust Through Specifics, Not Just Stars

Kept the quotes tied to a real detail — which variant, which job, why it fit their day — instead of generic praise. Large portraits and a dark background give it some editorial weight instead of feeling like a stock testimonial block.

07 -

Answering Doubts

Placed right before the final CTA on purpose. Fit, water resistance, warranty, returns — the stuff people actually hesitate on. Accordion format keeps it out of the way until someone needs it.

08 -

Staying in Touch, Not Just Selling

A soft newsletter signup for people not ready to buy yet, paired with simple footer navigation and the brand's Geneva address — a small detail that adds authenticity to a luxury product bought entirely online.

What This Project Taught Me

01

Restraint Reads as Luxury

Over-designing was the biggest early mistake. Removing excess color and motion let the product create a premium feel.

02

Trust is a Layout Problem

For a $299 purchase, trust comes from showing packaging, warranty, and reviews exactly when customers begin to hesitate.

03

Systems Beat One-Off Screens

Defining type and color tokens early made every section faster to build and feel like one cohesive product.
