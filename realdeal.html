RealDeal – Property

Product Overview

  • REALDEAL is a luxury real estate platform for discovering and reserving ultra-premium residences in Dhaka's most prestigious neighborhoods.
  • The project explores how a real estate brand can feel as prestigious as the properties it sells, without burying buyers under listings and paperwork.
  • The experience guides users from a curated first impression, through property discovery, neighborhood insight, and advisor trust, toward booking a private viewing.
  • The design balances heritage-driven elegance with the clarity a serious property decision demands.
Role UI/UX Designer
Client RealDeal Properties
Duration 2 Weeks
Live Site Realdeal.Design

Problem Statement

Luxury property sites often show beautiful photography, but leave buyers unsure how to actually narrow down a decision this significant.

The challenge was to create an experience that could:

  • Make the brand feel exclusive without feeling cold
  • Let buyers filter and compare properties without friction
  • Communicate neighborhood value beyond just square footage
  • Build trust in the people behind the transaction
  • Move serious buyers toward booking a viewing, not just browsing

Core Problem

How might we help buyers evaluate a multi-million dollar property with the same confidence as a much smaller purchase?

Possible Solution

A curated, advisor-led landing page pairing refined visual storytelling with a clear path to a private viewing.

Key solution areas included:

  • A search-first hero so serious buyers can filter immediately
  • Featured properties with essential specs upfront
  • Neighborhood breakdowns that sell the area, not just the address
  • Advisor profiles to put trusted faces behind the brand
  • A direct booking form as the final, low-friction next step

This approach allows serious buyers to filter and evaluate quickly, then feel confident enough in the people and the place to book a private viewing.

User Research

Focused on what makes a luxury property buyer trust a listing enough to book a viewing.

  • What makes a listing site feel credible for high-value property?
  • How do buyers compare neighborhoods, not just individual homes?
  • What information do they need before requesting a viewing?
  • Who do they want to speak to — a form, or a person?

Key Findings

Prestige Needs Proof

Buyers expect polish, but numbers like awards, residences delivered, and satisfaction rates do more convincing than imagery alone.

Neighborhood Sells the Home

Buyers evaluate the area as much as the property — safety, access, and lifestyle context matter alongside square footage.

Faces Build Trust

For a transaction this large, buyers want to know who they're dealing with, not just a company name.

Filtering Saves Face

Buyers don't want to scroll past homes outside their budget or type — early filtering keeps the experience feeling tailored, not generic.

Viewings Beat Forms

The real goal isn't a lead-form submission — it's getting a serious buyer into a private viewing conversation.

Response Speed Matters

Buyers researching multi-million dollar property expect a fast reply — slow follow-up can kill interest, even when the property is a perfect fit.

User Persona

Represents a typical RealDeal buyer: established, discerning, and evaluating a property as both a home and an investment. This persona helps define the user's motivations, expectations, goals, and pain points throughout the digital shopping experience.

Goals

  • Find a residence that reflects his status and taste.
  • Understand a neighborhood's long-term value, not just its look.
  • Compare a shortlist of properties efficiently.
  • Speak directly with a trusted advisor before deciding.

Pain Points

  • Listings scattered across generic portals lack context.
  • Hard to judge a neighborhood without living there.
  • Uncertain who's actually handling the transaction.
  • Booking a viewing often feels like a cold, impersonal form.

Typography

The typography system pairs Playfair Display for section titles with Manrope for body content — the serif brings the heritage and prestige a luxury property brand needs, while Manrope keeps listings, specs, and forms clean and easy to scan.

Used Colors

The RealDeal color system uses a restrained luxury palette where deep charcoal neutrals create space, while a warm golden accent provides the primary visual identity — reinforcing the exclusivity and heritage the brand represents.

The wireframing stage established the landing page structure, content hierarchy, and user flow before moving into visual design. It helped organize search, listings, and neighborhood content clearly, creating a seamless path from property discovery to booking a viewing.

User Interface

The final landing page pulls everything together — heritage-driven typography, a restrained charcoal-and-gold palette, and a clear path from discovery to booking.

01 -

Search First, Trust Immediately

Led with a full-bleed property shot and filter panel in the hero, letting serious buyers narrow by type, price, and location before scrolling. The stats bar — 180+ residences, 37 awards, 98% satisfaction — builds credibility before listings even appear.

02 -

Two Homes, Full Context

Rather than a dense grid, only two featured properties are shown at a time, each with price, size, and specs upfront — giving each residence room to breathe instead of getting lost in a list.

03 -

What We Actually Do

Three clear service pillars — development, consulting, design — framed with icons and a short line each, so visitors understand the full scope beyond just buying a house.

04 -

Selling the Address, Not Just the Unit

Lifestyle, safety, and connectivity are broken into their own callouts over a map-style background, answering the real question buyers have: what's it like to actually live here?

05 -

Local Knowledge as a Selling Point

Listing counts per neighborhood, paired with 15+ years of bespoke service, position RealDeal as insiders — not just another agency listing the same public properties.

06 -

Putting Faces to the Brand

Advisor portraits and titles replace an anonymous "our team" page, so buyers know exactly who they'd be working with before ever picking up the phone.

07 -

One Form, One Clear Ask

The closing section skips a generic contact form for a single, direct request — name, email, preferred neighborhood — making the next step feel like booking a viewing, not submitting an inquiry.

What This Project Taught Me

01

Prestige Isn't Decoration

Relying too much on dark visuals weakened trust. Real numbers and neighborhood details made the brand feel credible, not just expensive.

02

Trust Needs a Face

FLarge purchases need more than listings; showing the actual advisors buyers would work with helped reduce hesitation and build trust.

03

Context Sells the Address

Locking neighborhood storytelling alongside property specs meant every listing felt like part of a place, not just a unit with a price tag.
