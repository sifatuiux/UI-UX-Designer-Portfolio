Verdura – Plants

Product Overview

  • Verdura is an indoor plant e-commerce landing page designed to bring the calm, natural feeling of a plant shop into a clean, welcoming digital space.
  • The project explores how a plant brand can feel alive and inviting online, while still helping first-time buyers pick a plant they can actually keep alive.
  • The experience guides visitors from an inviting nature-led hero, through curated collections and best-sellers, into the reasons to trust the brand, toward a first order.
  • The design balances soft, organic visuals with the practical reassurance a plant purchase — a living thing — actually needs.
Role UI/UX Designer
Client Verdura
Duration 3 Weeks
Live Site Verdura.design

Problem Statement

Buying a plant online feels riskier than most purchases — it's a living thing, and first-time buyers often worry it won't survive their home, let alone the delivery.

The challenge was to create a digital experience that could:

  • Make the brand feel warm, natural, and calming rather than clinical
  • Help beginners find a plant that actually suits their space and care ability
  • Reduce the fear of ordering a living product sight unseen
  • Build appetite appeal through rich, natural imagery
  • Move casual browsers toward their first confident order

Core Problem

How might we help someone with little to no plant experience feel confident choosing, ordering, and caring for a plant online?

Possible Solution

A warm, nature-first landing page that pairs lush plant photography with a simple, guided path from browsing to a confident first order.

Key solution areas included:

  • A soft, nature-led hero that sets the calming tone immediately
  • Curated collections grouped by plant type and care level
  • A best-sellers section to guide undecided first-time buyers
  • A clear "Why Choose Us" section to answer care and trust concerns upfront
  • A limited-time offer and real customer reviews to close the decision

User Research

The research focused on what makes a first-time buyer comfortable choosing and ordering a living plant online.

  • What makes someone trust that a plant will arrive healthy?
  • How do beginners decide which plant matches their space and skill level?
  • What visuals actually make a plant feel appealing online?
  • What convinces someone to order again after their first purchase?

Key Findings

Care Confidence Matters Most

First-time buyers hesitate most over whether they can actually keep a plant alive, not over price or looks.

Visuals Sell the Plant

Rich, close-up photography of healthy plants does far more to build desire than descriptive text alone.

Curated Collections Reduce Overwhelm

Grouping plants by type and care level helps undecided shoppers narrow down choices faster.

Offers Nudge the First Order

A visible, time-limited discount pushes hesitant first-time buyers from browsing to ordering.

Real Reviews Build Confidence

Feedback from real customers, especially fellow beginners, reassures buyers more than brand claims.

Care Guidance Extends Trust

Visible care tips and easy-care labelling reassure buyers the brand will support them after checkout too.

User Persona

The user persona represents a typical Verdura shopper looking to bring more greenery into her home without prior gardening experience. This persona helps define the user's motivations, expectations, goals, and pain points throughout the digital shopping experience.

Goals

  • Find easy-care plants that suit her home's light and space.
  • Quickly understand which plant fits her balcony versus her living room.
  • Get plants delivered safely, without damage in transit.
  • Feel confident her plants will thrive without expert help.

Pain Points

  • Plant care terminology online often feels technical and confusing.
  • Hard to tell if a plant will suit her home's climate and humidity.
  • Worried about fragile plants arriving damaged from delivery.
  • Struggles to find trusted reviews from other home gardeners like her.

Typography

The typography system pairs Lisu Bosa for section titles with Merriweather Sans for body content — Lisu gives Verdura a calm, elegant, editorial feel, while Merriweather Sans keeps product details, care tips, and forms clean and easy to scan.

Used Colors

The Verdura color system uses a natural, plant-inspired palette — a deep, calming green carries the brand's identity across sections, while soft cream and white neutrals keep product photography and forms feeling light, airy, and uncluttered.

Mid-Fidelity Wireframe

The wireframing stage focused on defining the landing page structure, content hierarchy, and browsing flow before applying the final visual design. It helped establish a clear path from the hero through collections, best-sellers, and trust-building sections into the final offer and reviews.

User Interface

The final landing page pulls everything together — an elegant serif wordmark, lush plant photography, and a calm green-and-cream palette. The sections below walk through the actual designed screens, section by section.

01 -

A Calm First Impression

A soft green gradient frames a bright product shot of the plants themselves, with an elegant serif headline setting a calm, homely tone before any scrolling happens. A single "Shop Plants" CTA keeps the first action simple.

02 -

Browse By Type, Not By Overwhelm

Plants are grouped into clear collections — Cactus & Succulents, Blooming Greens, Perfect Bonsai, Climbers & Vines — each with its own photo and one-line description, so a beginner can narrow down without scanning a full catalog.

03 -

Letting Popularity Guide the Undecided

A best-sellers grid gives undecided shoppers a shortcut — plants other buyers already trust — with a simple "View more" link keeping the page from feeling like an endless catalog.

04 -

Answering "Why Should I Trust This?"

Four short trust points — air purifying, easy care, eco-friendly, decorative — sit right above a 25% limited-time offer carousel, addressing hesitation and giving a reason to act in the same scroll.

05 -

Closing With Real Voices

Customer testimonials with photos and star ratings sit above an open feedback form, letting new visitors read real experiences while inviting existing customers to add their own — reinforcing trust right before the page ends.

What This Project Taught Me

01

Living Products Reassurance

Unlike most e-commerce, a plant purchase carries a "will it survive?" worry that has to be answered before checkout, not after.

02

Curation Beats a Full Catalog

Grouping plants by type and care level made browsing feel guided rather than overwhelming, especially for first-time buyers.

03

Calm Design Sells Products

Soft gradients, natural photography, and unhurried pacing did more to communicate the brand's promise than any single line of copy.
