OCULYN – VR

Product Overview

  • OCULYN is a conceptual e-commerce experience for a VR hardware brand, designed to sell headsets, controllers, and accessories to buyers and serious VR enthusiasts.
  • The project explores how a tech-forward, immersive product can be sold online through a website that feels as futuristic as the hardware itself.
  • The experience guides users from a bold first impression, through gear discovery, product comparison, real-world use cases, toward a confident purchase.
  • The design balances high-energy visual identity with straightforward product decision-making in a single-page experience.
Role UI/UX Designer
Client Oculyn VR
Duration 3 Weeks
Live Site Oculyn.Design

Problem Statement

VR is a category people are cautious about. It is still unfamiliar to most buyers - expensive, spec-heavy, and easy to feel lost in.

The challenge was to create an experience that could:

  • Make VR feel exciting, not intimidating
  • Present hardware categories clearly
  • Translate specs into real benefits
  • Show real use cases
  • Build confidence through reviews and clear answers

Core Problem

How might we sell a high-tech, unfamiliar product in a way that feels thrilling to explore but easy to decide on?

Possible Solution

An energetic, motion-driven landing page pairing an immersive hero with clear product guidance underneath.

Key solution areas included:

  • Bold hero that puts users into the feeling of VR
  • Simple 3-category gear split (Headsets, Controllers, Accessories)
  • Featured products for quick comparison
  • Performance section translating specs into benefits
  • Use-case cards to help users picture themselves using it

This approach allows users to first feel the excitement of VR and then receive the information needed to make a confident decision.

User Research

Focused on what moves someone from curious about VR to actually buying it.

  • What stops people from buying VR?
  • What do they understand about specs like latency or FOV?
  • What proves comfort before they've tried it?
  • What role do reviews play?

Key Findings

Excitement Sells First

Motion and visual energy convince before specs do, creating an immediate sense of immersion, possibility, and desire.

Specs Need Translation

Technical terms like "low latency" mean little without a clear outcome that shows how they improve the user's experience.

Comfort Is a Real Concern

Long session comfort is a recurring hesitation, especially when buyers are unsure about weight, fit, and overall wearability.

Use Cases Build Confidence

Buyers commit faster when they can picture a specific use, whether that means gaming, fitness, entertainment, or exploration.

Compatibility Is a Silent Blocker

Uncertainty around device compatibility can quietly kill purchases, even when the product itself feels appealing and well-designed.

Peer Proof Matters

Real user reviews and experiences often outweigh brand claims, helping buyers feel more confident about the product before making a decision.

User Persona

Represents a typical OCULYN customer: curious about VR, technically comfortable, not yet an early adopter. This persona helps define the user's motivations, expectations, goals, and pain points throughout the digital shopping experience.

Goals

  • Find gear that fits how he wants to use it.
  • Understand performance in plain terms.
  • Trust comfort and compatibility before buying.
  • Read real user opinions.

Pain Points

  • Specs are inconsistent across sites.
  • Comfort is hard to judge from photos.
  • Compatibility info is often unclear.
  • Marketing language feels overhyped.

Typography

The typography system balances a bold, tech-forward tone with clean readability. Open Sans anchors section titles with confident, geometric weight, while Inter handles the interface for clear, consistent scanning across dense product and spec content.

Used Colors

The OCULYN color system uses a bold, tech-forward palette. Violet and blue create an energetic, futuristic identity, while a clean neutral scale keeps dense product and spec content easy to read.

User Interface

The final landing page pulls everything together — clean typography, a restrained color palette, immersive imagery, and a clear path from discovery to purchase.

01 -

Step Into the Experience First

Led with a full-bleed hero showing someone mid-use rather than a static product shot. The neon accent trail under her hands gives the whole page its visual signature before a single spec is mentioned.

02 -

Three Doors, Not One Maze

Instead of one big catalog, gear is split into three clear categories right after the hero. Anyone landing here knows in two seconds whether they're shopping headsets, controllers, or accessories.

03 -

Compare Before You Commit

A horizontal product row with price, spec, and a direct buy button lets users size up the lineup without opening five tabs. The carousel dots keep it from feeling like a wall of cards.

04 -

Turning Specs Into Feelings

Numbers like latency and refresh rate don't mean much on their own, so each stat sits next to a plain-language benefit — comfort, speed, compatibility — with a single hero image anchoring the middle.

05 -

Showing, Not Just Telling

Five use-case tabs (Fitness, Gaming, Education, Simulation, Movie) let people picture VR in their own life instead of imagining a generic gamer. The glowing grid images do the selling, the labels just point the way.

06 -

Real Voices Over Star Counts

Testimonials are tied to a name, a role, and a specific reason it worked for them — not just five stars and a quote. It's a small trust signal that matters more for a product people haven't tried in person.

07 -

Clearing Doubt Before Checkout

Placed right before the final push, this FAQ answers the practical hesitations — compatibility, comfort, technical setup — so nobody has to leave the page to find out if OCULYN fits their life.

08 -

One Last Nudge

A focused closing CTA with a single striking image — no more convincing needed at this point, just a clear "buy now" or "talk to sales."

09 -

Closing the Loop

The footer folds in a newsletter signup, quick links, and a soft, oversized OCULYN wordmark glowing through a headset outline — the last brand beat before someone leaves the page.

What This Project Taught Me

01

Energy Needs a Leash

Too much neon glow weakened the impact. Limiting it to key accent moments made each section feel more exciting and intentional.

02

Comfort Is a Trust Problem

For an unfamiliar, expensive product, trust came from explaining technical features as clear, relatable benefits—not making bigger promises.

03

One System, Every Section

Defining the dark palette and glow tokens early made every section faster to build and kept the entire product visually consistent.
