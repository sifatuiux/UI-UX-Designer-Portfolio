Dhaka – Coffee

Product Overview

  • DHAKA COFFEE is a modern café landing page designed to bring the warmth of a neighborhood coffee shop into a clean, inviting digital experience.
  • The project explores how a small local café can feel just as crafted and considered online as the coffee it serves, without losing its cozy, unpretentious charm.
  • The experience guides visitors from an inviting first impression, through the menu and specialty offerings, into the atmosphere of the space itself, toward visiting it.
  • The design balances rustic warmth with the simplicity a quick, everyday decision like grabbing a coffee actually needs.
Role UI/UX Designer
Client Dhaka Coffee
Duration 2 Weeks
Live Site DhakaCoffee.design

Problem Statement

Most local café websites either look generic or try too hard to look premium, losing the everyday warmth that makes a coffee shop feel welcoming.

The challenge was to create an experience that could:

  • Make the brand feel warm and inviting, not corporate
  • Let visitors quickly find a drink that matches their mood
  • Communicate the in-store atmosphere before they even walk in
  • Build appetite appeal through visuals, not just menu text
  • Move casual visitors toward exploring the menu or visiting in person

Core Problem

How might we help a small neighborhood café feel as inviting online as it does when you walk through the door?

Possible Solution

A warm, flavor-first landing page pairing appetizing visuals with an easy path to explore the menu and visit in person.

Key solution areas:

  • A flavor-first hero so visitors immediately sense the mood
  • A "Find Your Flavor" section letting people pick a drink by taste, not just name
  • Specialty coffee highlights beyond the usual classics
  • Atmosphere visuals that sell the in-store experience
  • A simple visit and contact section as the final, low friction next step

User Research

Focused on what makes a casual coffee drinker choose one neighborhood café over another.

  • What makes a café's website feel inviting rather than generic?
  • How do visitors decide what to order before they even arrive?
  • What kind of visuals actually make coffee look appetizing online?
  • Do visitors care more about the drink, or the atmosphere?

Key Findings

Flavor Guides Decisions

Visitors don't just want a menu, they want a quick way to match their mood to a drink — smooth, bold, sweet, or strong.

Visuals Build Appetite

Warm, close-up photography of drinks and beans does more to sell a coffee than descriptive text alone.

Atmosphere Matters as Much as Taste

Many visitors choose a café for the vibe as much as the coffee — cozy corners and warm lighting are part of the decision.

Offers Drive First Visits

A visible discount or limited-time offer nudges undecided visitors from browsing to ordering.

Real Reviews Build Local Trust

Testimonials from real, local customers reassure first-time visitors more than generic praise.

Location Ease Closes the Loop

Visitors expect the location, contact, and map details to be easy to find without digging.

User Persona

The user persona represents a typical Dhaka Coffee visitor: casual, flavor-curious, and choosing a café as much for the vibe as for the coffee itself.

Goals

  • Quickly find a drink that matches her mood.
  • Get a sense of the café's vibe before visiting.
  • Discover new flavors beyond her usual order.
  • Book a table without calling or waiting in line.

Pain Points

  • Café websites often show pretty photos but no real menu detail.
  • Hard to tell if a place is cozy or crowded before showing up.
  • Limited time offers are usually buried or hard to find.
  • Booking a table usually means a phone call, not a quick click.

Typography

The typography system pairs Lisu Bosa for section titles with Quicksand for body content — the handcrafted warmth of Lisu Bosa gives the café brand a personal, inviting feel, while Quicksand keeps the menu, descriptions, and forms rounded, friendly, and easy to scan.

Used Colors

The Dhaka Coffee color system uses a warm, café-inspired palette — soft cream and blush neutrals create a cozy backdrop, while a rich coffee-brown accent carries the brand's identity, reinforcing warmth and handcrafted comfort throughout the experience.

Mid-Fidelity Wireframe

The wireframing stage established the landing page structure, content hierarchy, and user flow before moving into visual design. It helped organize the flavor finder, specialty highlights, and visit section clearly, creating a seamless path from discovering a drink to visiting in person.

User Interface

The final landing page pulls everything together — handcrafted typography, a warm cream-and-brown palette, and a clear path from discovering a flavor to visiting in person. The sections below walk through the actual designed screens, section by section.

01 -

Warmth From the First Scroll

The hero pairs full bleed coffee photography with a bold serif headline and softly floating bean and leaf illustrations, setting a handcrafted tone before any content even loads. A single "Explore Menu" CTA keeps the first action simple.

02 -

Pick a Flavor, Not a Product

Rather than a plain menu list, four core drinks — Americano, Cappuccino, Latte, Espresso — are shown with their own photo, mood driven description, and "Grab a Cup" button, letting visitors choose based on taste rather than scanning a full menu.

03 -

One Offer, Full Focus

The Latte Love section highlights a single seasonal offer with a bold 20% discount badge, giving visitors a clear, low effort reason to act now instead of just browsing.

04 -

Beyond the Classics

Specialty Coffees showcase Mocha, Flat White, Cold Brew, and Affogato in a clean photo grid, signaling that Dhaka Coffee offers more than the everyday order.

05 -

Real Faces, Real Trust

Customer testimonials pair real photos with short quotes and star ratings in a simple carousel, building local credibility without needing a full review platform.

06 -

One Easy Next Step

The closing section combines location, contact details, and a short message form side by side, plus a map in the footer so visiting or reaching out feels like a two second decision, not a chore.

What This Project Taught Me

01

Warmth Isn't Just Color

The early drafts leaned on browns and creams alone to feel cozy. Real warmth came from photography and tone of voice, not just palette.

02

Simplicity Sells Coffee

Visitors didn't need a dozen options up front. Narrowing the first choice down to a flavor made the decision feel easy, not overwhelming.

03

Atmosphere Is Part of the Menu

Photographing the space itself, not just the drinks, ended up doing as much convincing as the coffee itself.
